Starting a Research Profile Before a PhD

By A.M. Rinas Published October 10, 2026 Category: Academic 7 min read
By A.M. Rinas Published October 10, 2026 Category: Academic 7 min read

Building a research profile before applying for a PhD can strengthen an application and provide clarity on research interests. This post documents the process of identifying research gaps, reading papers, designing experiments, writing results, finding collaborators, and maintaining an academic profile.

Research Gaps

The first step is to survey the literature in a specific domain and identify gaps where knowledge is lacking or conflicting. A research gap might be: "No existing study compares the effectiveness of open-weight LLMs on Sinhala NLP tasks," or "Limited research on real-time scam detection in Sinhala messaging platforms." Gaps are documented in a literature matrix with columns for author, year, methodology, key finding, and identified gap.

Paper Reading

A structured reading workflow includes: scanning the abstract, reviewing the methodology, evaluating the results, and noting limitations. Papers are saved with tags for domain, method, and quality rating. A reference manager (Zotero or Mendeley) is used to organize the library and generate citations.

Experiment Design

If the research gap requires empirical investigation, an experiment design is created. This includes: research question, hypothesis, variables, data collection method, analysis technique, and expected contributions. The design is reviewed by a mentor or peer before implementation.

Writing & Publication

Writing begins early—notes from paper readings are transformed into draft sections, and results are written as they are obtained. A target venue (workshop, conference, or journal) is selected, and the paper is structured according to its guidelines. Peer review feedback is incorporated iteratively.

Collaboration

Finding collaborators involves reaching out to professors, posting on academic networks (ResearchGate, LinkedIn), and attending research seminars. Collaborators can provide feedback, access to datasets, and co-authorship. It is important to establish clear roles and expectations early.

Academic Profile

Research Profile Example

  • Publications: 2 conference papers, 1 workshop poster
  • Presentations: 1 departmental seminar, 1 international conference
  • Dataset: Open-source dataset released for Sinhala NLP research
  • GitHub: 5+ repositories with code and documentation
  • Talks: 2 invited talks at local meetups

Responsible Research Outreach

  • Ensure all contributions are accurately credited.
  • Share data and code open-source when possible.
  • Follow ethical guidelines for research involving human subjects.
  • Disclose conflicts of interest.
